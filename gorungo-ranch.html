Our story

Hari Dasi's legacy

Hari Dasi was a young deer, grievously injured and missing a foot. Our neighbors came together with remarkable kindness. In her final hours, we placed a Bhagavad-gita against her body. After she died, we gave her a gentle resting place on our farm — and let new life grow in her memory.

We named her Hari Dasi on the disappearance day of Haridasa Thakura, who devoted his life to chanting the holy names of the Lord — the Sri Caitanya-caritamrta describes him chanting Krishna's names 300,000 times each day, and departing at the end of his life while chanting the name of Sri Krishna Caitanya.

We could not take away Hari Dasi's injury. But we could stay beside her, honor her life, and give her a resting place. The care our neighbors showed her inspired us to build something lasting.

GoRunGo Ranch is a memorial greenhouse in the Kiowa and Elizabeth area. Families commemorate a beloved pet or person by adding living greenery — a plant, a tree, a garden — to a sanctuary that also shelters and protects cows. A visit is a chance to remember, give thanks for a life shared, and watch something grow in their honor — and revenue helps us build the greenhouse, grow the gardens, and care for our cows. Through this place, Hari Dasi's short life can help make compassion a lasting, living part of our community.

Their memory becomes cow-seva

A beloved animal's memory, turned into care for another

When a companion animal passes, their love doesn't have to end. A GoRunGo Ranch memorial turns that love into lifelong care for cows who need shelter, food, and safety — one animal's memory helping sustain the peaceful life of another.

For families who understand compassion through karma and seva, this memorial can be a loving offering: one animal's memory helps sustain the peaceful life of another. GoRunGo Ranch does not guarantee spiritual outcomes; it provides a transparent opportunity for compassionate action.

Land & county planning

A low-impact, land-respectful plan

GoRunGo Ranch is a memorial greenhouse on our 35-acre Agricultural property. Greenhouses are a permitted use in the Agricultural district, and most of the land stays open pasture and ranch, with the cows kept by right. We commemorate a beloved pet or person by adding living greenery — a plant, a tree, a garden — grown and tended within the greenhouse.

Only a defined portion becomes the memorial greenhouse; the rest stays our residence, pasture, and ranch. Every tribute is a living planting, tended with care and named in our records — a growing remembrance that commemorates a life by adding greenery to the sanctuary.

Ranch progress

What exists today — and what we hope to build

We begin with what already exists. The renderings below are architectural visions, not construction promises — each is clearly labeled a concept and paired with a real photo of the property as it is now.

The Living Memorial Greenhouse & Cow Sanctuary

A living greenhouse where cows shelter and graze on the enclosed ground level — warm and protected through winter, with doors that open so fresh air can blow through on fine days — while clear upper floors let sunlight reach every level to grow gardens. The frame is modular, so growing levels are added over time. Throughout, living memorial plantings — each dedicated to a beloved pet or person — grow among the gardens, around a library, a place of learning, and a space for seva. Most of the 35 acres stays open pasture.

Each memorial is a living, growing tribute — tended within a greenhouse continuously filled with devotional and yogic hymns, so the sanctuary is bathed in sacred sound for the life of the building.
